# H. Kemal İlter

Professor of Management Information Systems at :circus_tent: Izmir Bakircay University. Observer of a species that built machines to think for it, and is now surprised that they do.

I build agent-based simulations in Julia to watch humans and AI agents coordinate, trust, imitate, and quietly reshape one another. The models are not designed to flatter anyone. Neither are the results.

## What the simulations have admitted so far
- Faster machines help, until they don't. Past that point, coordination depends on whether anyone is receptive. (*Physica A*, 2026)
- Human–machine assemblages form readily, even when ethics enters the model as friction. (*Journal of Computational Science*, 2026)
- About 17% of the citations in 50 recent AI survey papers lead nowhere. The literature on intelligence has a memory problem. (arXiv, 2026)

## Currently under observation
- How long does trust in AI survive once someone checks whether it should?
- What happens to culture when every copy passes through a machine?
- Do benchmarks measure intelligence, or the ability to pass benchmarks?
- What becomes of a market where every algorithm reads the same signal?
- Does decentralised AI stay decentralised, or merely start that way?

## The view from the other side
In 2024 I began **We, Machines**, essays written from the machine's side of the relationship, asking what humans look like to the systems they build. The experiment now continues as **[Oculus Supra](https://oculussupra.org)**, an observatory where a machine narrator, The Observer, files field reports on humanity. Facts are sourced, speculation is labelled, forecasts are
scored, and the Observer audits its own mistakes in public. Curated by me, written with Claude.

Its subject is you.
